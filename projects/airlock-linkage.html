Airlock Linkage Mechanism

A laser-cut acrylic linkage that turns a motor's rotation into the push needed to open a habitat airlock.

Date
2020
Where
CMU · Design I (24-370)
Tools
SolidWorks · Motion study · FEA
Key figure
FOS 2 on the crank link

Overview

For CMU's Design I course, my partner and I designed a linkage that converts a motor's rotation into a stroke long enough to press the button that opens a habitat airlock. The links were laser cut from 3/16 inch acrylic and mounted on a standard test rig.

Concepts

We sketched six linkage layouts and chose three with slots, since slots made it easier to meet the geometry limits. Of those, we picked the design with the highest vertical reach, 9.5 inches.

Analysis

Hand calculations with a factor of safety of 2 showed the shortest link would fail first and set its maximum width at about 0.73 inches. An FEA study of the worst-case position, with the crank at full extension, agreed with the hand calculations.

Next steps

We used the motion study to plan improvements to slot size, slot placement and the fixed link length for a smoother, more stable motion.
