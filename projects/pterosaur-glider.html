Pterosaur-Inspired Glider

Adaptive wing mechanisms modeled on pterosaur flight, designed for dynamic flight stabilization.

Date
2026–now
Where
CMU Robomechanics Lab
Tools
SolidWorks · XFLR5 · Isaac Lab
Key figure
Fixed and retractable wings

Overview

Graduate research at the CMU Robomechanics Lab on gliders with fixed and retractable wings inspired by pterosaurs. The goal is a wing mechanism that can adapt in flight to stabilize the glider.

Mechanism design

I designed the adaptive wing mechanisms in SolidWorks and prototyped them with 3D printing and machining. The onboard electronics had to fit inside the glider body within a fixed mass budget.

Aerodynamics

I am analyzing pterosaur-inspired wing sections in XFOIL, XFLR5 and AVL, with CFD validation, to estimate the loads the retraction mechanism has to carry. Glider tests are planned to validate and refine the design.

Learning-based control

In parallel I am building physics simulations in Isaac Lab and applying reinforcement learning to wing control policies, with aerodynamic constraints built into the simulation.
