Pterosaur-Inspired Glider

A tendon-driven wing-retraction joint for a bio-inspired morphing-wing glider, built and compared across 3 rapid prototypes.

2026–now
Where
CMU Robomechanics Lab
Tools

Key figure
3 rapid prototypes

Overview

Graduate research at the CMU Robomechanics Lab on a morphing-wing glider inspired by pterosaurs. The goal is a wing that can retract and adapt in flight to stabilize the glider and control its landing.

Mechanism design

I'm designing a tendon-driven wing-retraction joint in SolidWorks, comparing tendon and linkage concepts across 3 rapid prototypes made with 3D printing and machining. I also select the actuators and integrate them with the onboard electronics and embedded control, all within a strict mass budget.

Aerodynamics

I am analyzing pterosaur-inspired wing sections in XFOIL, XFLR5 and AVL, with CFD validation, to estimate the loads the retraction mechanism has to carry. Glider tests are planned to validate and refine the design.

Control and landing

I'm optimizing landing trajectories and morphing-wing control for the glider. In parallel I'm building physics simulations in Isaac Lab and applying reinforcement learning to wing control policies, with aerodynamic constraints built into the simulation.
