MoonRanger Rover Chassis

A 24 lb carbon fiber chassis for an autonomous lunar rover, packed into a suitcase-sized footprint.

MoonRanger mission site ↗

2021
Where
CMU · MoonRanger team
Tools

Key figure
24 lb chassis

Overview

MoonRanger is a suitcase-sized autonomous microrover from Carnegie Mellon's Robotics Institute, built to search for ice near the Moon's pole by measuring hydrogen along its route. With no radio link to Earth, it has to sense terrain, navigate and map on its own. I was on the chassis team, which designed its carbon fiber chassis to stay light and stiff through launch and the extreme temperature swings on the lunar surface. MoonRanger is now slated to fly to the Moon in 2029 on Firefly Aerospace's Blue Ghost lander through NASA's Commercial Lunar Payload Services program.

Design

Carbon fiber gave the chassis a high stiffness-to-weight ratio while handling extreme thermal expansion. The finished chassis came in at 24 lb.

Packaging

Every electronic component and sensor had to fit in a suitcase-sized volume. I built 3D models of the internal hardware and iterated the CAD layout until fit and clearance worked.

Validation

I ran more than 20 stress simulations in ANSYS and SolidWorks covering launch vibration and lunar terrain loads to confirm the structure would survive.
